01 / THE FIRST PRINCIPLE

Information becomes
a place to encounter.

The shape of a dataset suggests a kind of country. Its source, structure and history determine what belongs there.

I
TABULAR / QUANTITATIVE

Mountain ranges

A table or time series gets a summit. Declared, normalized variation becomes relief; related datasets form a range. Higher never means truer, better, or more valuable.

II
RELATIONAL / NESTED OBJECTS

Forests of knowledge

Relationships gather in valleys. Nested collections grow on wooded foothills. A tree can hold a traceable record; its canopy can reveal a family of objects.

III
TRANSFORMATION / PASSAGE

Information rivers

Follow information from a table into a graph or an object collection. Every authored route names its transformation. A drawn connection is never evidence of causality.

02 / A PLACE OF YOUR OWN

Your boathouse.
An open horizon.

A data lake is somewhere you can actually go. Walk down to a personal boathouse, launch a boat, and navigate between record buoys, collections and new questions.

Boathouses are the proposed boundary for personal collections and access. This public prototype demonstrates navigation with metadata. Private storage, identity and permissions are future work.

03 / HISTORY HAS A SHAPE

A landscape that
remembers.

❋

Glaciers retain history.

Archive caps hold the idea of versioned snapshots. A layer is a moment you can return to. Old does not mean wrong; a glacier represents retained history.

↯

Avalanches reveal change.

An update can cascade through dependent collections. An authored avalanche makes that propagation visible. It does not predict a market crash or a physical disaster.
